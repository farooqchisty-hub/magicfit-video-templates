MagicFit Video Template Engine: plan for review

200 product-agnostic 30 second video ad templates across 10 styles. Input: template file + product URL. Output: a captioned 30 s ad. Drafted 29 Sep 2026.

1. What a template is

A template fixes the concept (world, cast, story spine, beats, camera, music map, caption look) and leaves the product open. At run time the agent enriches the product URL and writes the dialogue, voiceover and product moments to fit the fixed concept. Example: the F1 driver's day-in-the-life stays; the shampoo becomes whatever the URL sells.

What makes "any physical product" work is an adaptation contract inside every template:

  • Product archetype resolved from the URL: worn, applied to body, ingested, handheld tool or gadget, home or furniture (too big to hold), carried accessory, consumable or refill.
  • Per archetype, each product beat says where the product appears, how the character uses it, and at what scale (a racer holds a serum but sits on a sofa).
  • Lines are intents, not words: "hook: the driver admits the one thing that slows him down, which is {pain the product solves}". The concept stays fixed and the wording is generated.
  • Claims only from the product page. No invented stats and no medical claims beyond what the page states. Health and supplement products get a stricter claims mode.
  • A fit score. If a product fits the concept badly, the agent says so and suggests better templates instead of forcing it.

2. Three layer rule system (so learnings update all 200 at once)

LayerHoldsCount
Runner (AGENT.md)Pipeline, product enrichment, QA gates, caption rules, stitching, the house rules from the cinematic harness (native sound first, one continuous score, phonetic brand words, physics, fictional world)1
Style (style.json)What makes anime look like anime: visual grammar, camera language, caption look, model prompt vocabulary, known failure modes10
Template (template.json)Concept, cast cards and reference images, world, beats, line intents, adaptation contract, unit plan200

Templates inherit from their style and the runner. A learning from a test goes into the lowest layer it applies to, so it reaches every template without editing 200 files.

4. The runner (what the buyer's Claude or Codex does)

  1. Enrich the URL: Shopify /products/<handle>.json, schema.org JSON-LD, then a page scrape as fallback. Build a product card: name, brand, phonetic spelling, archetype, 3 benefits quoted from the page, use moment, scale, images, allowed claims.
  2. Fit check against the adaptation contract. Stop and suggest alternatives if the score is too low.
  3. Screenplay: fill every line intent in the template's concept, with delivery notes. Brand words spelled phonetically.
  4. Keyframes: image model with product image + cast cards, one per beat. This becomes the buyer's own contact sheet.
  5. Video units: 2 to 4 generations totaling about 30 s. Exact-risk lines (brand, CTA) go in the short unit.
  6. Assemble: frame-exact stitch, one continuous score, loudness at -14 LUFS.
  7. Captions (mandatory wherever there is speech): word timings from the final vocal stem, burned in using the style's caption look, plus a .srt file.
  8. QA: transcript vs script, brand word check on the vocal stem, frame scan (faces, product fidelity, physics, real-brand leaks), runtime 28 to 32 s.

Buyer requirements to state on the listing: an agent with a video model (Seedance class), an image model, and ffmpeg. The template is model-agnostic; model tips live in the style layer.

6. Phases, gates and task list

Phase 1: Research the top 10 styles no gate

  1. Pull D2C video ads from Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center top ads (treg / ScrapeCreators), plus the CPMA library's mined video patterns.
  2. Classify each ad by style; rank by prevalence and persistence (completed lifespan and live age kept separate, as in Phase 0 of the ad agent).
  3. Grade AI feasibility per style and write style.json v0 for the top 10 (visual grammar, camera, caption look).

Phase 2: 10 flagship storyboards for one example product

  1. Pick the example product (see open decisions) and run the enrichment step on it, which also tests step 1 of the runner.
  2. Write one flagship concept per style, already in the template schema, so extracting the template later is mechanical.
  3. Per concept: 30 s beat sheet, screenplay with dialogue and VO, cast cards, unit plan, music map, caption look.
  4. Generate 8 to 10 keyframes per storyboard (image model, product image + cast refs) and render 10 contact sheets on one Pages site: panel, timecode, line, camera, product moment, caption sample.
CHECKPOINT 1: you review and approve the 10 contact sheets.

Phase 3: Reverse-engineer into the unified template

  1. Freeze the schema, write AGENT.md (the runner), write 10 style.json files and 10 template.json files from the approved boards.
  2. Dry-run adaptability on paper: run the runner up to the screenplay and keyframes for 3 very different products (worn, ingested, furniture) on 3 templates. Costs a few dollars; no video.
CHECKPOINT 2: approve the template format and the paper adaptations.

Phase 4: First real video and iteration

  1. Hand one template + the product URL to a clean agent session that only has the runner, and let it produce the 30 s captioned ad. This tests the product as a buyer would get it, not my tooling.
  2. Review, diagnose, fix at the right layer, rerun. Two or three rounds.
CHECKPOINT 3: approve the first video.

Phase 5: Scale to 200

  1. Write 19 more loglines per style (190), checking for diversity across worlds, archetype coverage and casting.
CHECKPOINT 4: approve the 200 loglines (cheap to change here).
  1. Expand all 190 into full template.json files with cast cards, then run a schema lint and the adaptation check (every template must resolve all 7 archetypes or explicitly mark ones it excludes).

Phase 6: Random tests and rule sweep

  1. Pick 4 or 5 random templates across styles and products and produce real videos.
  2. Fold every learning into runner, style or template rules; re-lint all 200.
CHECKPOINT 5: approve the bundle for packaging.

Phase 7: Package and host

  1. Bundle: AGENT.md, manifest.json, styles/, templates/<id>/ (template.json, cast/, preview.mp4 for tested ones, contact_sheet.png for all).
  2. Cloudflare R2 as the source of truth; one Google Drive folder per template mirrored from R2 for buyers.
  3. Also templatize the 69 existing MagicFit homepage videos into the same schema as a bonus pack.

7. Why test before scaling to 200

The first real video will almost certainly change the schema (unit lengths, how line intents are phrased, what the adaptation contract needs). Writing 190 templates before that means rewriting 190. So the order is 10 flagships, then test, then 200, then the random tests.

9. Open decisions for you

  1. Example product for the 10 contact sheets. Default: one mid-size item from a real Shopify store that is visually clear and category-neutral (a skincare serum or an insulated bottle). Send a URL if you have one.
  2. Aspect: 9:16 only, or also 1:1 / 4:5? Default is 9:16.
  3. Order: OK with testing before scaling to 200 (section 7)?
  4. Top 10: research-ranked, or do you want to lock certain styles (animation, comic, cinematic, creator) no matter what the data says?

House rules applied throughout: fictional worlds and blank signage, North American casting, native sound first, one continuous score, captions wherever there is speech, no em dashes.
